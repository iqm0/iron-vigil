# Last Light: concept reset

The walker that is in the game is abandoned as the hero design. It had no idea behind it: a yellow box with a tube for a gun, legs that did not look as if they carried anything, and a cannon that dominated the outline for no reason. Three rounds of refinement did not change that, because the concept was the problem.

This is concept exploration only. Nothing here is in the game. The sheets are plain grey masses, rendered with the game's renderer purely so that one column can show each concept **through the game's camera at the game's scale** (32 pixels to the metre, as on a 412-wide phone) and as a flat black shape. A person 1.8 m tall stands beside each for scale.

- [`mechs.png`](mechs.png): five walkers
- [`animals.png`](animals.png): three animals

Each row: front, side, three-quarter, the game's camera, the same as a silhouette.

**Nothing gets built until one of these is chosen.**

## The five walkers

### 1. ANVIL — the brute

**Pitch.** A quarry breaker's chest on a loader's legs: a hunched slab of a machine with the mechanic sunk in its chest and a forearm for each job.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Silhouette** | Wider than it is tall. A trapezoid chest that widens upward into two square shoulders, no neck, two heavy forearms carried low and forward like a boxer's guard. From above: a broad shield shape between two fists. |
| **Proportion logic** | About 5 m across the forearms, 4.3 m tall, 4 m deep. The chest and shoulders are well over half the mass. The legs are a third of the height and as thick as the forearms. The gun's barrels stick out less than a metre from the fist they are part of. |
| **Cockpit** | An open tub pushed out of the centre of the chest, bars across the front, a brow bar behind the mechanic's head. Head and shoulders show from the front and from above. The shoulders rise higher than the cab, so the machine looks as if it is hunched over the person in it. |
| **Legs and feet** | Short, reverse-kneed, thighs nearly a metre square. Feet 1.3 m by 2 m with two toe blocks. The stance is 2.6 m wide, so the feet show either side of the body from above. |
| **Weapon** | The right forearm *is* the cannon: a block the size of the fist, two fat barrels over and under, an ammunition drum for an elbow. |
| **Ram** | The left forearm is the pile-driver: a drum, a bright rod, a square striker plate 1.45 m across. |
| **Why it reads from the camera** | Dense and symmetrical in mass, asymmetrical in function. The two forearms point where it faces. The pale head in the cab is in the middle of the picture. Nothing thin. |
| **Risk** | The most familiar of the five. It needs one odd, specific thing to be ours and not a generic heavy robot: the floodlight bank on one shoulder and the open cab are a start, not enough. |

### 2. DOCKHAND — the loader

**Pitch.** A grain-terminal lift walker that never stopped being a forklift: two lifting arms carry a welded breach plate like a shield, and the gun rides on the roll cage where the beacon used to be.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Silhouette** | A T from above: a plate 3.3 m wide across the front, a cage behind it, a cast counterweight behind that, a small gun on top pointing wherever it likes. |
| **Proportion logic** | 3.3 m wide, 4.9 m tall, 4.6 m long. The plate and the counterweight are the two masses; the cage between them is open air with a person in it. |
| **Cockpit** | An open four-post cage high in the middle. The driver is the most visible pilot of the five: seated, in plain view from every side. |
| **Legs and feet** | Mid-length, on hips set out wide, square pad feet. Honest but not dramatic. |
| **Weapon** | A medium gun on a ring on the cab roof, turning on its own. The only concept where where-it-walks and where-it-shoots are two separately readable directions. |
| **Ram** | Both lifting arms thrust the plate forward. A shove, not a punch. |
| **Why it reads from the camera** | Plate, person, gun: three things in a row. The pilot is unmissable. |
| **Risk** | It is a forklift. Believable, and not exciting. The plate hides the front of the machine, and a roof gun is close to the tube-on-a-box we are leaving. |

### 3. HERON — the stilt hunter

**Pitch.** An orchard sprayer with its tanks stripped out: a small armoured pod carried high on long backward knees, a gun slung under it like a beak, a pick on a crane arm.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Silhouette** | Tall and narrow: a wedge pod over two long legs, three-toed feet splayed. |
| **Proportion logic** | 3 m wide, 5 m tall. The legs are two thirds of the height. |
| **Cockpit** | A caged nose at the front of the pod. |
| **Legs and feet** | Long, digitigrade, thin below the knee. |
| **Weapon** | A long gun under the chin. |
| **Ram** | A pick that comes down from above. A stab, not a ram. |
| **Why it reads from the camera** | It mostly does not. From above the pod is small and the legs are lines. |
| **Risk** | Flimsy, and the closest of the five to walkers people have seen before. Included because the brief asked for it; I would not build it. |

### 4. STRONGROOM — the bunker

**Pitch.** The farm's strongroom on legs: a welded armoured box with a slit to drive through and a hatch to fight from, a searchlight for a head, a gun built into its face and a cow-catcher across its shins.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Silhouette** | A block. From above, a hexagon with a lamp and a head on top of it. |
| **Proportion logic** | 3.4 m wide, 4.4 m tall, 3.5 m deep. Almost all hull. |
| **Cockpit** | Two reads: a lit slit across the front, and an open roof hatch with the mechanic head and shoulders out of it, like a tank commander. From above the pilot is right there on the roof. |
| **Legs and feet** | Very short and very thick, round elephant pads. Mostly hidden under the hull. |
| **Weapon** | A short fat gun through a ball mount in the front plate. The most integrated gun of the five. |
| **Ram** | A V-shaped prow shoved out on two rams. |
| **Why it reads from the camera** | One big clear mass with a person on top. The searchlight gives it a face. |
| **Risk** | It is a tank that happens to have legs, and from above the legs vanish: it would glide. The prow is muddled in the blockout. Its best idea, the mechanic fighting from an open hatch, could be taken into another concept. |

### 5. COMBINE — the harvester

**Pitch.** The front end of a combine harvester given legs: a glasshouse cab perched above a toothed header wider than the machine, which it drives into things.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Silhouette** | A wide toothed bar out in front, a tall cab behind it. Nothing else looks like it. |
| **Proportion logic** | The header is 4.6 m wide and leads the machine by two metres. Body 2.6 m wide, 4.4 m tall. |
| **Cockpit** | A tall open-framed cab, up and forward, the mechanic visible through it. |
| **Legs and feet** | Set back under the grain tank, thick, big feet. The body cantilevers forward over the header. |
| **Weapon** | A twin-barrel pod on the flank where the unloading auger hung. The weakest part: it is small and off to one side. |
| **Ram** | The header and its turning reel. The ram *is* the silhouette. |
| **Why it reads from the camera** | The toothed bar tells you which way it faces from any distance. It is the only concept that says "farm" without a caption. |
| **Risk** | The ram dominates the way the gun used to. It could read as a lawnmower. The gun needs a real answer. |

### My shortlist

1. **ANVIL**, as the review recommended. It solves the most problems at once: mass, legs that carry it, a gun and a ram that are limbs and not attachments, a pilot in the middle. It is also the safest, and the least distinctive.
2. **COMBINE** is the one with an idea nobody else has. If ANVIL feels generic to you, the route I would explore is ANVIL's body with a farm-machine signature: for instance the brute's ram forearm ending in a short toothed header section, or the mechanic riding high in a harvester's open cab above the chest.
3. **STRONGROOM**'s open hatch is worth stealing whichever way this goes.

I would drop HERON and DOCKHAND.

## The three animals

All three are near-black bodies with pale bone weapons, because the weapon is the identity.

### A. HOOK STALKER — two hooks and the legs to throw them

**Pitch.** A sprinter built round two sickles: all its mass is in its haunches, its back slopes down to a low head, and it carries a hook the length of its body on each side, held low and forward like ice picks.

- **Silhouette:** an arrowhead with two curved hooks ahead of it and two high knees behind. About 6 m from hook tip to tail, 2.4 m at the knee.
- **Attack identity:** it plants the hooks, sinks onto its haunches, and throws itself along the line between them. The hooks land first. It is committed, and afterwards the hooks are in the ground.
- **Why it works with the walker:** the wind-up is a change of outline (the haunches drop, the hooks open). The opening after the lunge is long and obvious. It is the natural partner for a ram.

### B. TAIL LANCER — one spear, carried over its back

**Pitch.** Low, wide and flat on six splayed legs, with a tail arched up and forward in four joints and a lance on the end of it pointing wherever it is looking.

- **Silhouette:** a flat shield of a body with a question mark over it. About 4 m of body, the lance tip 3.5 m ahead of the head.
- **Attack identity:** it does not come to you. It stops at five metres, the tail draws back, and the lance stabs down a line. A reach attack with a tell you can see from across the yard.
- **Why it works:** the most readable of the three from the game's camera; the tail is visible from every angle and tells you exactly where it is aimed.
- **Risk:** it is a scorpion. Familiar, though for good reason.

### C. MAUL — a skull with a body behind it

**Pitch.** All shoulders: a bone plate the width of the animal carried like a shield, two tusks, front legs like pillars and almost no hindquarters.

- **Silhouette:** a blunt wedge, widest at the front, pale in front and dark behind.
- **Attack identity:** it charges in a straight line and does not steer. Shells glance off the plate; it is soft behind.
- **Why it works:** the opposite of the stalker in every way, which is what a second enemy should be.
- **Risk:** as the *first* enemy it teaches standing still and shooting a wall. Better as the second.

### My pick

**HOOK STALKER** first, as the review suggested, with MAUL held as the second animal for later. TAIL LANCER reads best in the sheet, and I would not argue hard against it.

## Carried forward, not acted on

Two independent acceptance reviews of the last build both said "not accepted". Their findings hold whichever walker is chosen, and belong to the build phase:

- **The ram had no wrong moment.** It crushed anything under half health, it was invulnerable for longer than the pounce lasted, and it paid out even on a circling animal. A ram should only crush something that is pinned, and should bounce off something that is not.
- **Shell hits did not read.** A half-metre shove on a three-metre animal that is already running is invisible. Hits need to stop it, turn it, or take something off it.
- **A tap on the ram went wherever the thumb was pointing**, which after a sidestep is sideways. A tap should go at what the cannon is tracking; a flick should override.
- **The lane lines read as the walker's own laser sight**, because both bodies aim at each other.
- **Weight:** 2.8 footfalls a second is a patter; the hull reached full speed before a foot finished its first swing; the chassis crabbed sideways while the hips turned.
- **The yard** was a grey slab with a house next to it; the fight sat in a band a third of the screen tall.

And from the latest review of the stage: wider playable space, the middle kept clear, a few meaningful things in a middle ring, the large structures pushed to the outside, the camera pulled back a little. None of that is touched until the walker is approved.
