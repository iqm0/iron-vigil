# Round two: three ANVILs, one HOOK STALKER, one shared scene

For review. **Nothing here is in the game and nothing in the game has changed.** These are blockouts: big masses, no detail, drawn with the game's renderer only so they can be seen through its camera. They are for approving identity, proportion and composition, not finish.

- [`round2-anvil.png`](round2-anvil.png): the three variations. Columns: front, side, top (a long lens, as near orthographic as the renderer gets), three-quarter, a paint proposal, the game's camera pulled back, and flat black shapes from that camera and from the front. A person 1.8 m tall stands beside each.
- [`round2-stalker.png`](round2-stalker.png): the Hook Stalker in two poses, stalking and winding up.
- [`round2-scene.png`](round2-scene.png): one shared scene in a phone's frame, with the camera as it is now and pulled back.

## What all three ANVILs share

- **Size.** About 5.1 m across the forearms, 4.7 m to the shoulders, 4.3 m across the feet.
- **Lower half.** The legs and pelvis are close to half the height. Thighs over a metre square, shins not much less, a hydraulic ram behind each knee. Feet 1.45 m by 2.2 m. The stance is as wide as the chest.
- **Chest.** A trapezoid that widens upward into the shoulders, hunched forward. No head: the shoulders are the highest fixed part.
- **Cannon.** The right forearm is the gun: a cradle the size of the fist with two fat barrels that stick out about 0.7 m. Shorter than the forearm it is part of.
- **Pile-driver.** The left forearm: a drum, two bright guide rods, a driving rod.
- **How they are attached.** Each shoulder turns on a trunnion over a metre across, with a gusset back to the chest and a lift ram from chest to forearm. These are the load paths; they are not decoration.
- **Wear.** One shoulder cover, one shin plate and one toe block are replacements in a different colour, and there is a welded patch on the chest.
- **Colour.** None of them is yellow. Each paint proposal is a farm colour, two tones and bare steel. Paint is a separate choice from shape: any scheme could go on any variation.

## The three variations

### ANVIL / CORNHEAD — harvesting

**The feature:** the pile-driver's striker is a section of corn header. Three long snouts go in ahead of the blow.

- **Silhouette:** one fist is a gun, the other is a three-pronged fork. The prongs are the longest, palest thing on the machine and point where it faces.
- **Pilot:** an open pulpit pushed out of the middle of the chest, bars in front, a roll hoop behind the mechanic's head and nothing above it. Visible in the top view and from the game's camera.
- **What it does for play:** the ram has a visible point. It is obvious which end hurts and how far it reaches.
- **Paint shown:** oxide red and cream.
- **Risk:** the snouts could read as claws or teeth, which pulls it toward "monster" and away from "farm machine". The feature is on a limb, so the body itself is the plainest of the three.

### ANVIL / HOPPER — grain handling

**The feature:** a grain tank on its back, full of shells, with the unloading auger carried over the shoulder to feed the gun.

- **Silhouette:** tall and asymmetrical. A flared tank stands above the shoulders and a pipe runs diagonally from it down to the gun arm. From the front it is the only one with a shape above the shoulder line; from above the tank is a large square.
- **Pilot:** the same open pulpit, in front of and below the tank.
- **What it does for play:** it shows where the ammunition is and how it gets to the gun, so the gun reads as part of the machine.
- **Paint shown:** faded green and ochre.
- **Risk:** it is the tallest and most top-heavy. From the game's camera, when it faces away, the tank is between the camera and the mechanic. And a pipe into a gun is close to what we just left.

### ANVIL / BONNET — a tractor that stood up

**The feature:** an old tractor's hood and radiator for a chest, with round headlamps either side of the grille, an upright exhaust with a rain flap, and the mechanic on an open seat *on top*, behind the hood, at a steering wheel, under a two-post roll bar. The shoulders are mudguards.

- **Silhouette:** three things reach forward: the gun, the snout, the pile-driver. A person sits on top between two arched fenders. It has a face.
- **Pilot:** the most exposed and the most visible of the three. He is the highest point of the machine, with nothing over him, and reads from every camera angle including from behind.
- **What it does for play:** the grille and lamps make "which way is it facing" unmissable, and the rider makes "there is a person in this" unmissable.
- **Paint shown:** dusty blue and off-white, with one fender off a red machine.
- **Risk:** the mechanic is very exposed, which is either the point (desperate, human) or absurd. The snout adds a third forward shape, so the front is busier.

### My read, for what it is worth

BONNET is the one I would not mistake for any other game's robot. It answers the pilot requirement best, it has a face without having a head, and its farm feature is the body, not an attachment. CORNHEAD has the best ram. If you want both, the corn-head striker fits on BONNET's pile-driver without changing anything else. HOPPER is the weakest from the game's camera.

I have not committed to any of them.

## HOOK STALKER, refined

About 9 m from blade tip to tail when stalking, of which the body is about 5; 2.4 m at the haunch, with knees that stand higher than its back. Its footprint is close to the walker's. Near-black, with bone blades and jaws as the only pale parts.

- **Six limbs, each with a job.** Two great hind legs in three segments (thigh, shank, foot), each thicker than a man. Two mid legs that hold the ground. Two arms.
- **The arms are limbs, not fins.** Each is three pieces on two visible joints: a thick upper arm from the shoulder to an elbow, a long forearm to a wrist, and the blade hinged at the wrist with a spur behind the hinge. The blade is about 2.3 m long and narrow.
- **Head.** A heavy wedge under a brow plate, three eyes down each side, and two jaws that open sideways.
- **Posture.** Low in front, high behind. The mass is in the haunches; the back slopes down to the head.
- **The wind-up is a change of outline.** Stalking, the arms are carried cocked and low with the blades pointing forward: a narrow arrow. Winding up, the haunches sink, the elbows go high and wide, and the blades hang open at about 8 m tip to tip: a wide M. The two front-view silhouettes on the sheet are that difference, with no colour and no marks on the ground.
- **Risk:** in blockout the joints are plain cylinders and it reads a little mechanical. That is a modelling matter, not a design one, but it needs care in the next phase or it will look like a robot spider.

## The shared scene

One walker, three stalkers (one stalking at about 12 m, one winding up at about 13 m, one approaching at 21 m), two people, a house, a shed and a silo as plain masses at the edge, a low fence, and a five-metre grid on the ground.

| | Now | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Camera distance | 21 m | 30 m |
| Ground width on a 412-wide phone | about 12.7 m | about 18 m |
| Scale | 32 px to the metre | about 23 px to the metre |
| The walker on screen | about 165 px wide, 40% of the width | about 115 px, 28% of the width |
| Room either side of it | 4 m | nearly 7 m |

At the proposed distance all three stalkers are in frame at once, the one winding up has its whole span on screen, and there are two grid squares of clear ground between the walker and the nearest animal. At the current distance two of the three are cut by the edge.

What the scene is arguing for, to be approved or not:

1. **The middle is empty.** Nothing stands within fourteen metres of the walker.
2. **Large things go at the edges.** The buildings start about thirty metres out.
3. **The camera is further back,** and the walker carries the frame by mass and colour, not by filling it.

What it does not show: lighting, materials, or any real environment. Those wait.

One thing I would change in it already: the bottom third of the frame is unused ground under where the thumbs go. The camera should look a little further ahead of the walker than it does here.

## Carried forward for the build, untouched

From the two acceptance reviews of the last build. None of this is acted on until the designs are approved.

1. **The ram has no risk and no wrong moment.** It crushed anything under half health in any state, it was invulnerable for longer than the pounce lasted, and it paid out on a circling animal. It should crush only what is pinned, bounce off what is not, and never be a shield.
2. **Shell impacts do not read.** A half-metre shove on an animal that is already running is invisible. A hit has to stop it, turn it, or take something off it.
3. **The ram goes the wrong way for a thumb.** A tap rammed along the stick, which after a sidestep is sideways. A tap should go at what the cannon is tracking; a flick should override.
4. **The walk is fast and light.** Nearly three footfalls a second; the hull at full speed before a foot finished its first swing; the chassis sliding sideways while the hips turned. Slower cadence, visible drop and sway on each step, speed tied to heading.
