## ANVIL

Decisions applied:

| Asked for | What was done |
|---|---|
| Keep Bonnet's body, rider, grille and fenders | Unchanged in layout: tractor hood and radiator for a chest, two headlamps, upright exhaust, the mechanic on an open seat on top at a wheel under a two-post bar, mudguards over the shoulders. |
| Replace the ram tip with a refined Cornhead striker | Done. |
| It must read as a farm-machine impact tool, not claws | Three snouts cut from about 1.6 m to about 1 m and widened from 0.6 to 0.85 m, so each is nearly as wide as it is long. They sit shoulder to shoulder with no gaps, on a steel crosshead 2.1 m wide that is tied back to the pile-driver's guide rods by two braces. Straight, blunt, steel-capped. It reads as a toothed wedge on a ram. |
| Preserve the broad, load-bearing lower body | Unchanged: legs and pelvis are close to half the height, feet 1.45 m by 2.2 m. |
| Blue and red paint, worn and patched | Dusty blue body, off-white hood top and grille bars, the left fender and the striker in red off a donor harvester (the striker now explains where the red came from). Mud-dark feet and shins. The gun's barrels and the front of its cradle are heat-darkened. One toe block and the chest patch are bare or mismatched. |

Two things I changed without being asked:

- **The hull now hunches forward.** In the last two rounds it was tipped slightly *back*; I had the sign of the pitch wrong. It now leans into its work by about six degrees, which is what was always intended.
- **A view from behind and a game-camera view facing away** are on the sheet, because the camera spends half its time behind the walker. The rider is visible in both.

A blockout cannot show chipped paint, rust or dirt gradients. What is shown is which panels differ and where the dark goes. The wear itself is a modelling and shading job.

## HOOK STALKER

Rebuilt from rounded masses with flat plates laid over them, so it reads as shell over muscle.

| Asked for | What was done |
|---|---|
| Less blocky thorax and head transition | Haunches, chest, neck and head are one line of overlapping rounded masses from hip to jaw, with five plates lapping down the back. |
| Less "hinged machine arm" | The cylinder joints are gone. Upper arms and thighs are swollen in the middle and narrow at the ends; elbows and knees are small knuckles between them. |
| Blade grown, not bolted on | Each blade comes out of a sheath at the wrist, in a colour between body and bone. The spur behind the hinge is gone. |
| Head more predatory, less faceted | Long and low, a crest swept back over the neck, a heavy lower jaw, three eyes a side, jaws that open sideways. |
| Haunches muscular | Thighs are the largest single masses after the body. |
| Wind-up broad but tense and directional, not a crab posing | The blades no longer go out sideways. The elbows come up and the blades are held high over the shoulders, angled forward and in toward the target, with the body sunk onto its haunches and the head low. About 5 m across at the elbows (it was 8 m at the tips) and 4 m tall. From the front it is two raised picks over a low head. |

Stalking it is narrow, about 4 m at the hind knees, with the blades carried forward and low.

## Motion

Six moments of one stride, the ram in two poses, the stalker in three. **These are poses, not animation: they can show whether a moment reads, not whether the timing feels right.**

- **Walk.** Each foot is on the ground for three fifths of the stride. The hull drops as each foot lands and rises over it, shifts toward the standing leg, and the chest turns against the hips. The swinging foot lifts 0.8 m, heel first. The stride is 2.5 m per step.
- **Ram, wound up.** The hull sinks a third of a metre and rocks back, the feet set one ahead and one behind, the chest turns the pile-driver shoulder away, and the striker is drawn up and back with its rod pulled in.
- **Ram, landed.** The hull goes forward over the front foot and pitches down, the back heel lifts, the shoulder comes through, and the rod is out to its full 1.35 m with the snouts in the animal's neck.
- **Stalker, stuck.** After the pounce: blades in the ground to their sheaths, body stretched out flat behind them, head down, tail up. This is the pose the ram is for.

## The shared scene, and an honest correction about the camera

The instruction was: keep the pulled-back distance, look further ahead, reduce the dead ground at the bottom, preserve thumb space.

Drawing the joystick and ram button onto the frames shows something I missed last round: **the empty ground at the bottom of the pulled-back frame is where the thumbs are.** The walker's feet already sat just above the joystick. Moving the look-at point further ahead pushes the walker down under the thumbs, which I tried first and rejected.

So the correction is made by **tilting**, not by sliding:

| | Last round | This round |
|---|---|---|
| Distance | 30 m | 30 m |
| Angle above the ground | 46° | 38° |
| Walker on screen | just above the joystick | the same place |
| How far up the yard you can see | about 30 m | to the haze |

The walker stays where it was, clear of the thumbs. The top of the frame, which is where everything approaches from, now reaches the buildings and beyond, and the lower angle shows more of the sides of things, so both the walker and the animals have more height and volume. The bottom fifth of the frame is still ground. I do not think it can be reclaimed on a one-thumb portrait game; it can only be made to matter less.

Two costs of the tilt, to weigh:

1. Distances up the screen are more compressed, so judging how far away an animal is gets a little harder.
2. At this angle the far distance is in view, so the world needs a horizon treatment (haze, sky, far silhouettes). That is new work, and it is also the "distant scenery" that has been asked for since the first review.

## Carried forward for the build, untouched

1. **The ram has no risk and no wrong moment.** It should crush only what is pinned, bounce off what is not, and never be a shield.
2. **Shell impacts do not read.** A hit has to stop the animal, turn it, or take something off it.
3. **The ram goes the wrong way for a thumb.** A tap should go at what the cannon is tracking; a flick should override.
4. **The walk was fast and light.** Slower cadence, visible drop and sway, speed tied to heading. The walk blocked out here is built to that.
